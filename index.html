I am a PhD candidate at Carnegie Mellon University’s College of Engineering, specializing in Intelligent Engineered Systems and Society (IESS) within the Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering. I am co-advised by Dr. Burcu Akinci, Dean of the College of Engineering, and Dr. Christopher McComb, Professor of Mechanical Engineering and Director of the Human+AI Design Initiative.

My research centers on developing and evaluating AI-enabled decision-support systems for decarbonization, with a particular emphasis on agentic AI for the design of energy-efficient, low-carbon infrastructure. I investigate how AI can push the frontier of what engineers can design, and under what conditions gains in AI capability translate into more effective human–AI systems, sounder engineering decisions, and measurable environmental impact.

As Herbert A. Simon wrote in The Sciences of the Artificial,

“Everyone designs who devises courses of action aimed at changing existing situations into preferred ones.”

In my work, AI is never just a model. It is embedded in sociotechnical systems that shape how people make engineering decisions and put them into practice. The unit of design and evaluation is therefore the system as a whole. I examine how AI capabilities, human roles, and engineering workflows should be configured, what they can achieve together, and at what cost in energy and human effort.
